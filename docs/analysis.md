# Analysis, activity details and equipment

## Analysis history

The **Analyse** tab has four sections: Laufen, Radfahren, Belastung and Erholung (an old `#analysis/performance` link opens Laufen); single sessions are opened in the calendar (an old `#analysis/sessions` link lands there). Belastung shows one Garmin Connect acute-load chart, with the latest 14 daily readings or twelve calendar weeks (last valid reading per week, including the current week), and a separate session-RPE load chart (AU, daily or weekly sums of rated sessions) with the share of rated sessions, and a Kurz-/Langzeitbelastung chart of the same AU smoothed over 7 and 42 days (values after a warm-up, gap days without value; no fitness or injury risk, never compared with provider load; the Coach training report carries it as `smoothed_session_load`). Below them the group Alltagsaktivität shows Garmin Connect daily steps and floors climbed as bars (daily readings, or the daily average per calendar week), with the latest value in the title; days without a value stay gaps and the group is hidden without data. Garmin training status is imported for the configured sync window; missing or ambiguous device readings stay unknown. Coach context also keeps Garmin's current daily acute and chronic load and the reported acute-to-chronic ratio separate from its four-week aerobic-low, aerobic-high and anaerobic balance values and target ranges. These values retain Garmin provenance and freshness; ambiguous device values and missing fields stay unknown. Training focus follows the period selection (14 days or 12 weeks) placed above it, then permanently visible HF and power zone diagrams. Focus groups recorded Garmin activity loads by primary Training Effect, read from the stored Garmin activity records (the upgrade re-projects older records once), rather than deriving categories from zones. Zones sum recorded sport-specific durations across all sports over the selected training-focus period; missing measurements are not zero. The Woche section and its cumulative load charts are removed. Laufen and Radfahren each start with a compact table of that sport's current values, showing metric names and values without source or measurement-date labels; Radfahren includes the latest Garmin FTP power-to-weight value. Below it, each sport's "Entwicklung" group charts threshold pace or FTP and VO2max over twelve calendar weeks. Laufen adds running tolerance, a best-efforts table (fastest measured pace over 1, 3, 5, 7, 12 and 20 minutes and best time over 1 km and 5 km, last 90 days, with the experimental critical-speed line) and the race predictions. Belastung also charts the endurance score as a Garmin weekly history, including groupMap/groupAverage range responses, because it rates all sports' long-term training. FTP keeps every dated Garmin daily measurement, including multiple measurements in a week. Threshold pace uses the last valid weekly measurement; VO2max uses weekly medians. Every measured week has a point, including unchanged values; missing weeks remain gaps. A chart without any valid value is not drawn and not listed, sport groups without history are omitted, and current-value rows without a value are hidden (a single measured value keeps its note that no trend exists yet). Race predictions appear only in a compact distance/time table within the running section: 5 and 10 km as minutes and seconds (21:47), half marathon and marathon as hours and minutes (1:39 h). VO2max keeps the one decimal of Garmin's precise value. A compact cycling power profile shows the best measured average watts over 5 seconds, 1 minute, 5 minutes, 20 minutes and 1 hour from the last 90 days, combining outdoor and indoor rides in one value per duration. It reads only the local mean-maximal curve of locally stored full-resolution streams of any provider (Garmin FIT, Wahoo FIT); downsampled streams never contribute. These are observations, not forecasts; missing durations stay unknown. One line below the table shows the experimental Critical Power (outdoor and indoor rides separately) and outdoor-running Critical Speed model estimate from the best 3, 7, 12 and 20 minute efforts (cycling) or 3, 7 and 12 minute efforts (running) of the last 28 days (evidence card M4); it is withheld when fewer than four (cycling) or three (running) independent efforts exist or a stop rule applies, and it never sets FTP or zones. Cycling power-to-weight appears only in the current cycling table; running tolerance belongs to running development. Current values and each sport's development group can be collapsed independently; best efforts and race predictions collapse with running and the power profile with cycling. Endurance-efficiency cards, best activity windows and race-prediction history charts are removed. Weekly axes display DD.MM dates. Acute load and training time use bars with connected points at their tops; training time sums each session's moving time, a strength session's total time (rest between sets is part of the work), like the calendar card and week sum. A strength session's calendar details list "Gesamtzeit" before "Bewegungszeit". Every measured point has a value label, including repeated values; labels move vertically to avoid collisions. A section with no data at all shows one empty card with its cause and one action: "Anbindung einrichten" when neither Garmin nor Wahoo is connected, otherwise "Jetzt abgleichen" (the section refresh); the period selection stays available. Loading, failed and absent values look different: report and session-detail reads time out after 15 seconds into an error card with "Erneut versuchen", and absent values (power-profile durations, nutrition tiles) read "nicht erfasst" in muted text. Erholung shows daily sleep, HRV and resting heart rate over the last fourteen days, with an optional twelve-week weekly-average view. A completed session's calendar card has a "Details" toggle under its values; each local session appears once, whichever providers (Garmin Connect, Wahoo) recorded it, older sessions are reached by paging back in the week or month view without limit (weeks before the configured "Wochen zurück" load on demand), and "Feedback" beside "Mit dem Coach analysieren" opens the rating and session dialog. Opening "Details" shows all key figures, Garmin Connect-style area charts for heart rate, pace or speed, power, cadence, elevation and temperature, and for runs the running dynamics per second (ground contact time and balance, vertical oscillation and ratio, step length; the Coach reads only their averages), with a dashed average line and tap/drag readout (elevation instead shows its full minimum–maximum range without an average line), recorded HF and power zone times labelled with their provider (without provider zone times, as with the Garmin Personal API, they are counted from the heart-rate and power streams with the zones in effect on that day and labelled "Zoneneinstellungen" followed by their source: "Garmin-Zonen" (the zones set on the watch, used as they are, also when they are based on the lactate threshold or the maximum heart rate), "lokale Zonen" or "Standardzonen"), and laps. Walking and hiking use pace like running. Pace sports invert the pace axis; other ranges ignore the outer one percent of samples so single spikes do not flatten a curve, only non-negative channels start at zero, and stops or missing samples stay gaps. Simplified charts and per-channel data coverage are disclosed below the charts. Running, walking and hiking show step frequency (both legs, spm), twice the stored per-leg cadence; other sports keep per-leg cadence in rpm. Charts need locally stored activity details. Without them the summary is shown, and a configured Garmin Connect offers an explicit detail-loading action. Load time, a changed summary and data coverage are noted per provider; while any provider's details are outdated, the session analysis is withheld. The expanded panel, its session analysis and the Coach read only Garmin, Wahoo and local detail. The session analysis and the recorded calendar workout profile are stored once per session and recomputed when its details, records, targets or thresholds change, so the calendar and the recurring comparisons show the same values, also for Garmin- and Wahoo-only sessions.

When the athlete changes weight or body fat in **More → Profile**, saving also attempts to send both saved values as one dated Garmin body-composition entry. Both values must be valid and Garmin must be connected. The profile is saved locally even when Garmin is unavailable or rejects the entry; an uncertain outcome is reported without automatic retry and the athlete should check Garmin before trying again. For a known unavailable or rejected upload, the profile offers an explicit retry. A concurrent profile change may supersede a pending upload. If Garmin remains busy or authentication takes too long, the upload does not start later. Coach updates and undo do not send entries to Garmin.

The **Körper** section under **Nutrition** starts with a two-row table labelled "Körperwerte". Weight and, when no profile value is set, body fat show the latest Garmin Connect measurement and date. A body fat value entered in the profile takes precedence and is labelled "Profil"; it has no measurement date and does not add a point to the history chart. The history charts use Garmin Connect observations only. Cycling power-to-weight appears as one value in the current-values table in **Analyse › Radfahren**, using Garmin FTP divided by a measured weight from the preceding seven days. Body offers the latest 14 days or twelve rolling weeks. Independently of that selection, the power-to-weight value in Performance shows the latest Garmin FTP quotient from twelve rolling weeks. There is no power-to-weight chart. Weekly points use the latest dated reading of the week; a single weekly reading is still drawn as a point. Observation dates remain available, and W/kg values keep FTP and weight provenance separate. Unmeasured dates and weeks remain gaps; profile values do not fill missing observations.

Erholung shows the latest fourteen days including today without a period selector, using three aligned charts with independent scales for sleep duration, nightly HRV and resting heart rate. Daily sleep is shown as bars starting at zero, with an average line; hours are formatted as hours and minutes. HRV and resting heart rate show the quartile range of the athlete's own earlier values (42 days, per source) only when at least 14 comparable earlier measurements exist; provisional ranges and insufficient or stale comparisons remain explicit. A change of measuring device, firmware, algorithm, sensor, window, protocol or method restarts the comparison with the values since that change; the info names the change date, or "Messkontext unbekannt" when the provider reports no device model. Every day retains its original observation; missing days remain gaps, and no missing measurement counts as zero. Each metric uses one source and measurement method: the freshest series wins, with Garmin preferred on equal dates. Current sleep, HRV and resting heart rate, their 7-day averages and the planning days keep Garmin's night values. Current weight and body fat take the most recent measurement across Garmin, the profile and Wahoo; within one day a device measurement wins, and other sources' values stay visible as alternatives. The Körperwerte table under Nutrition keeps its own rule described above. Under **Mehr → Profil → Quellen & Coach → Bevorzugte Quelle** the athlete can prefer Garmin Connect for weight and body fat (default: the newest measurement); the choice is saved with the profile (receipt and undo) and applies to the Coach, plans and charts. Thresholds, HRV, resting heart rate and sleep have no choice there: Garmin already leads them, behind a locally confirmed zone configuration. Current recovery values and the comparison ranges are read from stored canonical observations only; a value that exists only in a raw provider payload is not shown. Garmin's weekly HRV average is shown as the current HRV only when no last-night HRV exists, labelled "Garmin Connect (Wochenmittel)" and without a 7-day average. Sources, measurement dates and coverage remain available in tooltips; tap or keyboard-activate a day for details, or open the original-value table. Chart legends show only metric names. Values, dates, sources, coverage, changes and explanatory notes are available in legend and point tooltips; expandable tables retain the original values. The comparison range is a neutral background band. Recovery bands update with new observations; load and performance charts do not show comparison bands. Mixed-unit secondary axes have been removed. A position outside the range, a deviation from earlier values and a positive TSB are neither a medical statement nor a training clearance.

Charts preserve provider provenance and observation dates. Numeric labels are reduced when they would overlap, without removing data points. Tap a day or open Werte ansehen for complete values. Sleep shows minimum and maximum below the plot and its average beside the dashed line. HRV and resting-heart-rate comparison bands use earlier 42-day matching measurements, with at least fourteen observations (provisional below 28). These thresholds are product rules. The band holds the middle half of the athlete's own earlier values, so a value outside it is neither rare nor abnormal; it is not a normal range or a medical or sport-wide target. Where Garmin provides them, the Erholung tab also shows dated training readiness as its first chart; weekly training load is the acute-load chart in the Belastung tab. Missing values remain gaps. Historical availability depends on the sync window. Charts are read-only and never trigger provider requests.

## Activity details

On **Geplant → Übersicht** (Planned → Overview), "Details" opens a completed
session with its locally stored measurement series and intervals; "Mit dem Coach analysieren" opens the Coach and asks it to analyse that session (one turn started by the athlete, named by date, sport, time, duration and its id). The app loads a
session's details from the provider read-only and stores them permanently in the
canonical data model; "Detaildaten laden" (load detail data) explicitly starts a background job that
makes read-only requests only. The action appears when Garmin Connect is set up.
Load time, a changed summary and data coverage are noted per provider; while a
provider's details are outdated, the session analysis is withheld. The detail
view, the session analysis and the Coach read only Garmin, Wahoo and local data.
While credentials are missing, a setup notice on all tabs links to Mehr › Anbindungen (More › Connections); it can
be dismissed for the browser session. On errors the previous detail data is
kept; normal synchronizations do not replace it. Missing sensor values stay
visible as data gaps. Charts and Coach context show at most 2,000 points per
measurement series; the full series are stored up to the supported limit of
172,800 points and 24 MiB per activity.

Equipment and maintenance has its own More section, separate from Profile. Coach-managed local equipment records are authoritative for lifecycle actions, including assignment, maintenance, archive and restore. Each item is a collapsible card; closed, it shows the name (Garmin-only items carry a Garmin tag), one status chip (Wartung fällig, Wartung ok, Ziel überschritten, Ziel unbekannt, Ziel ok or Ausgemustert), one line with the parent bike, distance and lifetime percent, and the lifetime bar. Archived items are kept in a collapsed "Ausgemustert (n)" group. Garmin inventory and per-item statistics supply linked usage readings and status context without taking over the local lifecycle. The Garmin sync also reads, per gear item and read-only, the activities Garmin lists for it (up to 1000, stored as `activity_ids` with the gear item in the Garmin gear rows; an item with more Garmin activities links only the 1000 Garmin returns. A failed or unsupported read keeps the item's last known list, marked stale; without one, the item links nothing, which means unknown rather than empty). While an item's list is truncated or stale, maintenance intervals counted from its sessions, which are hours and a component's distance, show partial coverage and no not-due result; a Garmin item's distance interval still uses Garmin's own total. The equipment read lists at most 2000 assignments, explicit ones first, but usage counts all of them. Equipment reads the canonical sessions, so a session from Garmin or Wahoo alone counts in usage, hours, distance, maintenance and sport derivation, and a session bundled from several providers counts once; an assignment is stored under the session id. Those Garmin activities are matched to their session and count as sessions and hours of the Garmin-linked main item without a manual assignment, once per session and without a sport check. An activity Garmin lists for several main items, for example a bike and a power meter, links to each of them, as in Garmin's own totals. An explicit assignment, including an explicit unassignment, always overrides every Garmin link of that activity. Garmin mileage still comes from the Garmin inventory total. A still-open imported item the athlete has not saved and that has no components takes the sport that at least three quarters of all its linked activities, unknown types included, and at least three of them name; an athlete-chosen or earlier sport is never replaced, and an item with components or an ambiguous mix stays open. Garmin's `maximumMeters` is a lifetime target used for target/progress display, not additional distance: it is never added to Garmin's usage counter or assigned activity distances. A progress bar appears only when a valid target is known. Failed Garmin reads retain the last successful inventory, while a successful empty inventory clears the mirrored Garmin list.

Calendar cards include a compact interval profile when explicit timed workout steps or current locally cached original samples are available. Width represents time, height represents intensity, and known zones supply colors. Recorded sample gaps remain empty; stale detail profiles are excluded. Zone limits follow the canonical zone configurations of the unit's sport (3 to 7 zones; indoor and virtual units use an indoor configuration when one exists) and fall back to the standard limits (power and pace as in Intervals.icu; heart rate Coggan's five zones at 68, 83, 94 and 105 % of LTHR, so everything above 105 % is Z5). Per metric, local zones valid on the unit's day win over the provider's zones; Garmin's watch zones lead the other providers, and the standard limits apply only without any configuration. Absolute watt or bpm targets are colored as an estimate from the sport's FTP or LTHR and the card says so; without that threshold they use neutral colors. No provider requests are triggered by rendering the calendar.

Under Profil → "Zonen & Schwellen" (Zones & thresholds), one page per sport (Radfahren, Laufen, Schwimmen) shows the thresholds on top and every zone kind below, and local zones can be saved per metric (power in % of FTP, heart rate in bpm with LTHR and maximum, pace in % of threshold pace) and optionally for indoor sessions only (`GET /api/zones`, `PUT /api/zones`). The thresholds box shows "Anaerob (LT2)" (FTP or threshold pace, and the threshold heart rate, from the current performance values with the date they apply from) and "Aerob (LT1)": the heart rate from the stored share of the threshold heart rate (measured, or estimated from heart-rate variability), else the upper limit of heart-rate zone 2 in effect, else 85 % of the threshold heart rate; the power or pace at the upper limit of zone 2 of the pace or power zones in effect, else 75 % of the FTP or 88 % of the threshold speed. A small yellow "i" on each row opens where its values come from (the provider of LT2; measured, heart-rate variability, zone 2 or a fixed share for LT1); Analyse lists the same values as "Aerobe Schwelle (LT1)" next to the "Anaerobe Schwelle (LT2)" rows and marks a value from zone 2 or a fixed share "Schätzung". Its "Bearbeiten" opens the first-threshold entry (measured bpm, measurement date, stored share and the estimate to adopt), which is saved with the profile. Each zone kind lists its zones with name, range and percent and its source ("Lokal gespeichert", "Garmin, gilt für alle Sportarten"); a kind without zones shows "Noch keine Werte – Anlegen". "Bearbeiten" opens the editor under its box, prefilled with the zones in effect today (local, else Garmin including its sport-free default set), or with the current FTP or threshold heart rate when the kind has no zones yet. Analyse › Laufen and Radfahren list the same LT1 values, a value from zone 2 or a fixed share with the "Schätzung" chip, and the Coach receives them with their origin. A save is valid from a chosen date (default today, never in the future), so earlier days keep their old thresholds; only the first configuration can apply "von Anfang an". Local zones take precedence, survive every synchronization, restart and backup restore, and are never transferred to Intervals.icu. The Coach sees the zones in effect with their source. Invalid input (too few or non-ascending limits, unknown units) changes nothing. Saving and the confirmed removal (`PUT /api/zones/remove`) show a receipt with "Rückgängig" (undo).

Above the form in "Zonen & Schwellen", a warning lists every threshold that local load and zones read (FTP for cycling and running, LTHR for cycling and running, threshold pace) whose effective value (local zones, else Garmin, else Wahoo) is older than eight weeks, with value, source and age; the closed section names the number of stale values in its summary line and the status strip links to it, but the section does not open by itself. A local value counts from `valid_from`, otherwise from the last save (saving unchanged values also renews it); a provider value counts from the day the provider reports for it. A provider value beyond its age limit (Garmin 180 days, Wahoo 90 days) is shown as "nicht mehr verwendet" (no longer used). If the Garmin SDK fails (a skipped login at once, or FTP and lactate-threshold requests failing in three consecutive syncs), every effective Garmin threshold is shown as "wird nicht aktualisiert" (not being updated), with the cause. Indoor rides use the cycling FTP unless a local indoor FTP is saved; only that saved indoor FTP is listed once it is older than eight weeks. Nothing is estimated. `GET /api/zones` returns the warning as `threshold_warnings`; the Coach receives it in the current performance context.

Training nutrition no longer has its own display, API or Coach action. The local change history also does not appear as its own view; recording and Coach undo remain. Below a Coach reply that changed local data, a receipt line with a check mark lists the changes; a toast announces each Coach write, started job or failure once. If the reply changed only profile, competitions, workout templates, activity feedback, sessions or nutrition diary entries whose changes are fully recorded in the history, "Rückgängig" (undo) reverts all of them together in one transaction after confirmation; if a record was changed further afterwards, everything stays unchanged. Intervals.icu and Garmin are not modified by this. The diagnostics export still contains technical events and Coach command status as well as the number of stored changes, without exposing their athlete content.

Analyse (Analysis) and Ernährung (Nutrition) can each be refreshed separately with the refresh icon in their header. Analyse loads limited Garmin and Wahoo data for the selected range, as far as the provider is set up; Belastung (load) and Erholung (recovery) use the selected period. Ernährung loads the selected day or period for the diary or history, Garmin body data specifically for Body, and only local entries for products. Nothing is written to Intervals.icu in the process. Running refreshes keep the displayed values; missing sources or incomplete activity windows are marked as a partial result.
