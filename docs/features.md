# Features

## AI Coach & Conversational Intelligence
- **Natural Language Coaching**: Conversational coaching without rigid trigger words, supporting natural phrasing, corrections, follow-up questions, and pronoun resolution across turns.
- **Selectable model provider**: OpenAI Responses (or a compatible endpoint) or Claude through the Anthropic Messages API, chosen under Coach & Modell, both with real-time SSE token streaming. Chat Completions and fallback between providers are unsupported; voice transcription uses OpenAI or an explicitly configured OpenAI-compatible server such as local Whisper.
- **Durable Turn Queueing**: Every chat request is persisted in a durable SQLite background queue before processing, enabling seamless answer recovery across network drops or browser reloads.
- **Permanent Fact Memorization**: Conversational profile updates save athlete preferences, equipment notes, and constraints to the durable profile when the athlete explicitly asks in Coach Chat; no separate UI confirmation is needed.
- **Sport-Specific Training Preferences**: The profile keeps general training preferences plus separate preferences for running, cycling, swimming, strength training, and mobility; the Coach saves sport-specific wishes in the matching field.
- **Training Model**: The profile selects the intensity distribution the Coach plans with: pyramidal (default), polarized, threshold-focused or sweet spot. The Coach changes it only when the athlete asks, with a change receipt and undo.
- **Calendar Notes**: Timed calendar events never block a plan write. A hard or long unit that overlaps a timed event or falls on a day with at least eight hours of events gets a `calendar_load` note, and one on a day with an all-day event a soft `all_day_event` note; the planning brief lists all-day events per date with their training-impact flags and never subtracts them. The planning brief is capped at 20,000 characters by dropping whole entries with an `omitted` note: far-future all-day and busy-minute days first, then planned sessions, planned distribution and strength weeks from the end, and the oldest completed weeks last. The profile holds no weekly plan; weekday times come from the stated availability and the calendar.
- **Server-Side Write Gate**: Every Coach write tool call is checked by impact, not by wording. In a turn started by the athlete's own message, reversible local edits run directly and appear under the answer as a receipt row with a check icon and a direct "Rückgängig" where fully tracked. A failed Coach turn (provider timeout, exhausted credit, …) shows its error at the reply itself with "Erneut senden", which sends the athlete's text again as a new turn; the global error toast is only for failures that are not shown in the chat. Deleting or archiving records, replacing or committing a whole plan, and changing more than 7 records in one turn (a failed call counts none, so its corrected retry needs no extra confirmation) additionally need a small model classifier to confirm that the athlete explicitly asked for exactly that in the same data area (a requested deletion of a meal never confirms deleting a race); otherwise, or when the classifier fails, the Coach asks one clarification question and nothing is changed. Undoing a creation deletes the record, so the Coach offers it as a confirmation card instead of running it directly. Intervals.icu writes need the athlete's message (or a short "ja" to the Coach's closing sync question) to name the sync with Intervals.icu or a sync verb, so planning words like calendar or conflict alone do not count, and a refusal ("nicht synchronisieren", "mache ich selbst") blocks the whole message; the classifier can never authorize them. A named sync runs without an approval card, and its receipt names each remote deletion it carries. Adaptive replans keep their preview approval: only the athlete's very next message after the published preview can apply it, and the classifier must confirm that this message accepts the previewed change as proposed (a bare "danke", an unrelated message, or a rejection that asks for a different change instead does not); an illness-pause sync to Intervals.icu additionally needs the named sync and is built from the stored preview. A turn makes at most 25 changes. Plan writes by the Coach (patch, change set, replace, library schedule, commit, and the approved adaptive replan, which is rolled back as a whole on a block) are also checked on the resulting plan: a run more than 10 % longer (distance) than the longest run of the last 30 days, a planned run or ride whose load (duration × intensity², from the workout steps against the thresholds in the Coach precedence: locally confirmed zones first, then the current provider values) is more than 10 % (warning 30 %) above the highest-load session of its sport in 30 days, a planned weekly run or ride load more than 20 % above the mean of the previous 3-4 weeks, hard run/ride sessions on consecutive days, heavy strength within 6 h of a hard session, no taper (less than 20 % lighter final 14 days) before an A/B race, and hard or 90+ minute sessions within 5 days (today included) of a reported illness, or sessions above the current stage of the return after an approved illness pause (today and tomorrow), plus a note when a planned week's low/moderate/high time (from the workout steps, run and ride only; a unit without parseable steps counts by its planned intensity, else by its session focus, marked approximate; other unknown time reported as uncovered) lies clearly outside the reference range of the chosen training model, and leg strength (legs, full body, plyometrics; only the number and intensity of sessions count, no tonnage or load): moderate or heavy leg strength less than about 24 h before a hard or long run/ride, more than 3 leg sessions or more than 2 heavy ones in a week, or none in a plan of 2+ weeks when the profile or season phase expects strength. A strength unit the Coach creates, moves or changes without `strength_structure`, `session_focus` and `strength_intensity` is also rolled back (`strength_incomplete`, never acceptable; a run or ride unit without `session_focus` is blocked the same way by `focus_missing`; untouched older units do not block other writes). Of the training rules only the illness rule blocks: such a write is rolled back and the findings return so the Coach corrects the plan, and only an explicit athlete wish lets it resubmit with `accept_plan_findings`, which is shown in the change receipt (the wish is asked only when the write actually produced the illness finding). The running week is judged as "completed + planned": its completed days count with the remaining planned days for the intensity distribution and the leg strength limit. The other rules rest on observational or practice-based evidence, so the plan is saved and they come back as notes with their evidence strength (a run or load spike of 10-30 % is a notice, beyond 30 % a warning) shown with dates and evidence in the change receipt. Completed load uses ride Normalized Power over FTP and run grade-adjusted pace over threshold pace, with a heart-rate fallback marked as less precise; "hard" comes from the unit's session focus ("Art der Einheit"), else the computed intensity, with text and keywords only as fallback. Load notes are extrapolated from distance/volume studies, not directly validated; missing or stale (no current provider value; local values do not age) thresholds or unparsed steps give a "Last unbekannt" note instead of a number. Strength takes no part because planned units carry no session-RPE. The Coach can justify them without repeating the receipt. Edits made by the athlete in the UI are not checked. Weekly and block plan requests get a `planning_brief` (last weeks per sport, short deterministic `lessons` from the last block review, planned intensity distribution, planned strength sessions by leg and intensity, longest run, next races, `training_model`, and the season phase (base, build, peak, taper, transition) with the weeks to the next A/B race, derived read-only from competition dates, with transition for 28 days after an A/B race; unknown without such a race). The plan playbook sets the emphasis per phase for plan_week, plan_block and adjust_week.
- **Request-Specific Context Projection**: Compact daily, activity and dialogue windows for routine turns; detailed local context and additional tools are available on demand, with conservative fallback for continuations.
- **Targeted Clarification Protocol**: Ambiguous coaching prompts generate a single, concrete clarification question stored locally across reloads and model switches until answered.
- **Atomic Training Changesets**: Plan updates, session moves, and workout creations use transactional revision tracking and object hashes to prevent race conditions.
- **Contextual Quick Actions**: Start buttons for the Morning Check-in and an "Analyse & Plan" menu: analyse the latest session (each interval against its planned target, recoveries, steadiness, and the comparison with comparable earlier sessions), review progress over 4/8/12 weeks or since the last test (one review that leads with performance at equal effort: output per heart beat of steady sessions and pace at a fixed heart-rate band, and names plan execution and subjective signals only as possible causes; descriptive and read-only), plan the next week, plan a 2–4 week block, or adjust the current week to what happened. Each action carries its own request kind, so the server adds the matching context and a sourced training-science playbook (intensity distribution from the profile training model, hard/easy spacing, strength with endurance, taper) to that turn only. Plans may combine running, cycling and strength, and schedule a run critical-speed test and a Zwift FTP ramp test about every 6 to 8 weeks; the Coach evaluates a test against the last one of the same protocol and recommends threshold updates without writing them.
- **Session Bundling**: Dual-recorded workouts (e.g., Wahoo and Garmin cycling files) are bundled into one session automatically; the provider copies stay stored and nothing is deleted at the provider.
- **Structured Error Recovery**: Robust recovery from provider rate limits and transient network errors with transparent diagnostic reporting and safe rollback of incomplete actions.

## Training Calendar & Workout Planning
- **Calendar Navigation**: The `Kalender` tab combines planned and completed training. `Analyse` contains performance metrics in Laufen, Radfahren, Belastung and Erholung; single sessions open in the calendar card with "Details"; paging back has no limit, and weeks before the configured ones load their sessions on demand; a sport filter narrows the shown sessions (see Weekly Calendar).
- **Weekly Calendar**: The calendar opens on the current week as a timeline (weekday and date column, today filled) with previous/next week buttons and the ISO week, within the weeks ahead set under Darstellung; the weeks back set there load at once, older weeks on demand. One compact row per session (sport icon, title, duration, distance; a zone bar only for a planned workout profile; completed and missed sessions are one slim row with time and status such as "✓ Absolviert · 101 %", "✓ Zusätzlich" or "✕ Nicht absolviert"; activity names show without the athlete's own place, the profile's weather location, as in "Emsdetten - ") and a fixed planned/completed grid for duration, distance and load. Planned sessions are dashed, completed ones solid and missed ones outlined in red; identical extra activities of a day share one row, and workout profiles come with a zone legend. Opening a session shows its details; on wide screens they appear as a detail pane beside the week, with a larger zone profile, and one session of the week is open from the start. A planned session lists its steps (zone, duration and the target range from the zones of its day, such as "110–128 bpm") instead of the workout notation. "Verschieben" on a planned unit offers the next seven days and "…" for a later date, both up to the last day the calendar shows; one tap moves the local unit (a unit already sent then waits for its sync), and the receipt offers "Rückgängig". "Mit dem Coach besprechen" opens the Coach with the planned session named in the message field for the athlete's own question; nothing is sent until the athlete sends it. A "Woche | Monat" switch shows the month grid (one dot per session, on wide screens its duration and the week's planned time); tapping a day shows its week below. Chips "Alle | Lauf | Rad | Kraft" below the switch show one sport in the week and month (a done session by its recorded sport); the week figures and month hours then count only that sport, the whole-week plan compliance line is left out, appointments stay, the week sync still covers all units, and the choice is remembered in this browser only. The calendar always opens on the week. Calendar appointments and markers are neutral day notes; a planned session that conflicts with them shows one conflict notice whose "Mit Coach lösen" action asks the Coach for an adjustment. "Zusätzlich" marks extra activities only in weeks with a plan. Illness and pain from saved check-ins appear as day notices; days without sessions remain visible. Matched sessions show their share of the plan (load, duration or strength sets) in the status; opened, a completed session shows its chart and each value once per sport (runs: Pace, Ø Puls, Schrittfrequenz, Ø Leistung, Belastung, RPE, Bodenkontaktzeit, Höhenmeter; rides: Normalized Power, Ø Leistung, Intensitätsfaktor, Ø Puls, Trittfrequenz, Belastung, RPE, Ø Tempo, Höhenmeter; other sports: Belastung, RPE, Intensität, Ø Puls, Ø Leistung, Höhenmeter; a value without data is left out), one slim bar of the time in zones (power zones for rides that have them, else heart-rate zones) with the minutes per zone and the zone source, the plan line, "Mit dem Coach analysieren" and "Details"; additional activities have no invented target percentage. When the calendar pairs a run or ride with a planned unit, "Details" starts with "Soll/Ist": the share of time inside each planned step's target (laps pair with the work steps, the steps between share the time between them, a steady run shares its whole time; heart rate over the step's second half, stops left out; green from 80 %, amber from 50 %, red below), a strip of the steps sized by duration, the "Zieltreue" score (time in target over all scored time), planned against actual minutes per zone in the plan's target metric, and each step's target band dashed on its pace, heart-rate or power chart.
- **Configurable Planning Horizon**: User-adjustable calendar display settings controlling past lookback and future planning horizons through the More tab.
- **Plan vs. Actual Pairing**: Intelligent pairing of planned workouts to completed activities using provider pairing IDs with a conservative same-day sport fallback.
- **Visual Volume Comparisons**: Accurate plan-versus-actual volume matching evaluated by training load (TSS) when available, falling back to moving or elapsed duration.
- **Unmatched Activity Display**: Prominent display of completed, unscheduled sessions alongside planned workouts without fabricating missing target metrics.
- **Full Workout Lifecycle Management**: Planned workouts are scheduled, moved, edited, archived, restored, and deleted through the Coach, each change with a receipt and undo. The calendar itself offers the session focus, the Garmin exercise choice and the sync button.
- **Target Period Scoping**: Conversational plan adjustments strictly scoped to requested date ranges, leaving surrounding weeks and existing training blocks untouched.
- **Daily Athlete Check-ins**: Stored check-ins track soreness, stress, motivation, session RPE, day form, illness, pain, available minutes, availability notes, free-form notes, a day status (unknown, rest, pause) and the tags travel, late meal and high stress. They are recorded through the Coach chat and feed readiness, planning, and calendar day notices. Saved check-ins stay in the local data and in exports.
- **Post-Activity Feedback**: Dedicated local feedback logging for completed workouts, capturing perceived exertion (RPE), equipment details, and workout execution notes. Feedback belongs to the canonical session and is stored under the local session it was written for (equipment assignments likewise), so splitting a bundle keeps it with that copy: the provider copies of one confirmed session show and edit the same feedback, and its session RPE counts once. Older feedback saved on different copies stays stored; the newest is shown and the others are listed as `conflicting_feedback`. Clearing removes the session's feedback entirely. An id that matches activities of several providers stays on its own, and deleting a provider copy keeps the feedback. Local session RPE is never mixed with the provider's RPE. The RPE is chosen from 0 to 10 in half steps, with the CR-10 anchors (Ruhe, Leicht, Moderat, Schwer, Maximal and others) on their steps; the time of each new or changed rating is stored, earlier ratings keep an unknown rating time. **Session-RPE load** (method `session-rpe-load` v2, evidence card `docs/performance-methods/m1-session-rpe-load.md`) is elapsed minutes × session RPE in arbitrary units (AU): the activity details show it per session (for example "403 AU · RPE 6,5 (Athletenfeedback) × 62 min"), Belastung shows it per day or week with the count of rated sessions, and the Coach reads it in the training report. Without a local rating the Garmin Connect watch RPE of the same session is used and labelled; disagreeing copies of one provider, a missing rating or a session with only a moving time give no value; a session whose elapsed time exceeds its moving time by more than 15 minutes or 20 % keeps its elapsed value and is marked as a long interruption. AU are never added to Intervals.icu or Garmin load. The same form marks what the session was (**Art der Einheit**: Training, Wettkampf, Pendeln, Einfahren, Ausfahren, or "Automatisch" for no marking). The marking is local and wins over Garmin's event type and over the race hint of a stored competition on every read (summaries, Coach, power-profile and critical-power efforts, plan compliance, race results): a marked race is linked to the competition of its day like a Garmin race, any other marking, training included, takes the session out of the race hint and removes its competition link (a training marking also keeps the other sessions of that day from taking the hint; mark the right one as race), and commute, Einfahren and Ausfahren sessions stay out of bests, curves and planned-session matching. The marking shares the receipt, undo and merge groups of the feedback rows, so undo restores the previous marking and link; deleting the notes and rating (also through the Coach) keeps the marking, and an activity that cannot be assigned to one session cannot be marked. The Coach sets it with `save_activity_feedback` (`purpose`, `null` removes it) only for what the athlete names. Saving or removing feedback in the activity details shows a receipt with "Rückgängig"; undo restores every stored row of the session as it was, full-length notes and hidden older entries included. Feedback keeps the session it was written for, so splitting wrongly merged sessions stays lossless.
- **Provider copy corrections**: The activity details list the provider copies bundled into a session under "Aufzeichnungen". The athlete can confirm the bundle, split one copy into its own session, or merge it with another session of the same day; the Coach can do the same on request. "Aktivität löschen" removes the activity with all its copies locally after a confirmation; a later provider delete or a new delivery of a copy does not bring it back. Each correction shows a receipt with "Rückgängig", and corrected sessions are never re-bundled by later syncs. A sync splits a copy off an unconfirmed session when it no longer matches, for example after an edit at the provider. Nothing is changed at Intervals.icu, Garmin or Wahoo.
- **Multi-Phase Season Periodization**: Long-term seasonal planning mapping base, build, peak, and taper phases (completed after the race) anchored to competition dates; a transition phase follows an A or B race for 28 days.

## Workout Library & Structured Formats
- **Local Workout Library**: Repository, sorted by sport and name, of reusable endurance and strength workout templates stored entirely in the local encrypted database.
- **Planning Only From the Library**: Every unit the Coach plans comes from a library template (`template_id`) and keeps its exercises or steps in order; a variant only scales sets, reps, loads, durations and targets, and the receipt names it as a scaled variant. Without a fitting template the Coach creates one first, in the same turn for a clearly described unit, and asks only when the unit is unclear. A Coach write that breaks this is rolled back (`template_missing`, `template_mismatch`); older units without a template can still be moved, archived or deleted, but their content changes only by switching them to a template. The expanded unit shows "Vorlage: <name>".
- **Template Lifecycle Management**: Reusable workout templates are created, modified, scheduled, archived, and deleted through the Coach; the library view lists them.
- **Strict Endurance Syntax Enforcement**: Rigorous validation of endurance steps requiring explicit duration or distance alongside defined intensity targets (e.g., `- 15m 50-70%` or `- 6km Z1 HR`).
- **Relative Target Calculations**: Automatic resolution of percentage-based targets against the athlete's current FTP, threshold heart rate, or threshold pace (local, Garmin Connect or Wahoo).
- **Native Repeat Block Formatting**: Standardized parsing for interval repeat blocks with mandatory preceding count headers, blank-line delimitation, and step-level recovery tracking.
- **Prose Support for Non-Endurance Workouts**: Flexible free-form text formatting for strength training, yoga, mobility, and core routines without rigid step constraints.
- **Provider Upload Verification**: Two-phase upload verification ensuring Intervals.icu parses step instructions, duration, and training load before marking a workout synced.
- **Rejection of Ambiguous Formats**: Pre-upload validation rejecting prose-only endurance entries, mismatched target types, and conflicting watt conversions.

## Intervals.icu Output
- **Output-only Intervals.icu**: Intervals.icu receives library workouts, indoor and virtual ride units (for Zwift), competitions, nutrition and sport settings only on the athlete's explicit synchronization. Its activities, wellness history and activity details are not read, and no setting turns that on; the app only reads back records that an explicit export wrote. The Intervals.icu card (More → Anbindungen) keeps the connection check. Release 2.0.0 removed the Intervals.icu ingest data stored by earlier releases.
- **Session Focus**: Every planned unit and library template can carry a local "Art der Einheit": run and ride recovery, easy, endurance, long, tempo, threshold, vo2max, anaerobic, race or test (ride also sweet_spot), strength legs, upper, full_body, core, plyometrics or mobility with an optional light, moderate or heavy intensity. The Coach sets it for every new unit, and the athlete changes it in the opened calendar entry; a derived value is offered next to its empty field as "≈ … übernehmen" and becomes set with one tap. A unit without focus shows a dashed, derived chip taken from its parsed steps, then from keywords such as Squats, Beintag or Schwelle. For strength an explicit term in the name (Oberkörper, Unterkörper/Beine, Ganzkörper, Core, Mobility) beats exercise keywords, negated mentions ("Beine schonen", "ohne Beine", "kein Beintraining", "nicht schwer") never count, and without a structure the intensity is derived from the text (schwer, moderat/mittel, leicht). The focus stays local: the Intervals.icu export is unchanged, and it is covered by change history, undo and backup.
- **Run Heart-Rate Caps**: Recovery, easy and long run units carry a local `hr_cap` that is stored in percent only: the cap in percent of the run threshold heart rate ("LSHF"), the lower bound of the exported range and the cap source (default, measured, or estimated). The bpm is never stored: wherever it is shown, checked or exported it is resolved from the threshold in effect on the unit's date (local zones, then the dated Garmin watch zone set), so Garmin's automatic threshold updates apply without editing a unit; without a known threshold only the percent is shown. The card reads "≤ <percent> % LSHF · <bpm> bpm · <source>". The cap values per run type live in one table (`HR_CAP_PERCENT` in `backend/activities/hr_cap.py`). A Coach write of such a unit is rejected by the plan check `hr_cap_invalid` when the cap is missing, above the effective cap or its lower bound or source does not match; the message names the percentages. The athlete may enter a measured first threshold in bpm (More → Profil → Quellen & Coach, or the profile API; the Coach reads it but never sets it); it is stored as percent of the threshold on its measurement date, and easy and long caps then follow it minus a fixed margin with recovery below them. Runs recorded with a chest strap that writes RR intervals (Garmin only when HRV logging is enabled for the strap) give an estimated first threshold (DFA-α1, research grade, never a lab value): each run stores only its result, the RR intervals are discarded, and the profile shows the median of the good runs of the last 60 days (at least three) as an estimate with quality and session count. "Übernehmen" stores it as the first threshold with the origin estimated (a receipt with undo; the cap source then reads "geschätzt", and a stored measured value is replaced only after confirmation); the Coach sees the estimate but cannot adopt it. For the watch, every heart-rate step, warm-up and cool-down included, is sent as one wide range from a low floor up to the cap, so the watch alerts only above the cap. Garmin gets it directly as bpm resolved at push time (see Runs to the watch), because a Garmin workout step cannot hold a percent of the lactate threshold HR; a capped run needs a known threshold HR, else it is refused before any write. Intervals.icu gets no run units. Wahoo gets the percent of the threshold with the plan header's threshold reference from Pacecraft's current thresholds. The analysis of a completed run with a stored cap judges it against that cap resolved for the unit's date.
- **Structured Strength**: A strength unit or template can carry a local `strength_structure` (up to 12 exercises with working sets, reps, optional kg (0 = bodyweight, no added weight), %1RM, RPE or RIR, rest, up to 5 warm-up sets with their own reps and kg, and a short note such as "nicht bis zum Muskelversagen"). The readable description is generated from it, free text stays the fallback, and a manual description edit drops the structure. Without a set intensity, `strength_intensity` is derived from it (heavy from 80 % 1RM, RPE 8 or 2 RIR) and leg involvement from the exercise names, both labelled derived. The Coach uses it for progression across weeks; plan checks still count only leg sessions and their intensity, no tonnage. Executed sets from Garmin (exercise sets, else the FIT file) are compared with it: a matched unit's compliance counts the planned sets done at the planned reps (exercises matched by their Garmin exercise category, unknown ones never count as missed), a completed session with a leg exercise counts as leg strength, and the planning brief lists the recent top sets per exercise for the next progression step. The activity detail shows every executed set (exercise, reps, kg, rest), and the calendar entry lists the executed sets per exercise next to the planned structure. Strength units are not sent to Intervals.icu; they reach the watch through the direct Garmin push.
- **Explicit Plan Synchronization**: Unidirectional push transferring locally approved indoor and virtual ride units (for Zwift) to the Intervals.icu calendar with stable upsert identifiers (type `VirtualRide`, category `WORKOUT`). Runs and strength go to Garmin, outdoor rides to Wahoo as library plans; the plan sync skips them with the per-unit result "Geht nicht an Intervals.icu: nur Indoor-Radeinheiten" and the Coach receipt counts them. Events the app wrote there earlier stay untouched, and only the app's own events of deleted or replaced units are removed.
- **Sync button per week**: Each calendar week shows one "Woche synchronisieren" button while a future unit of that week still needs a push or something the app left at a provider for that week waits for removal; unit cards carry no sync button. A click runs a replacing sync: it sends every indoor or virtual ride unit of the week to Intervals.icu that was never synced, changed, failed or went missing remotely (a run, strength or outdoor ride unit never waits for Intervals.icu and is not counted in its pending sync state), and every strength unit with a `strength_structure` and every run unit with step text whose current version Garmin does not have yet as a structured workout. It also removes the events, workouts and Wahoo plans of deleted or replaced units of that week, only by the ids the app stored, never anything the app did not create; an undated or earlier leftover belongs to the current week. A Coach sync of the units it just created, changed or replaced also removes the Intervals.icu events of deleted or replaced units, so Intervals.icu does not keep forwarding a replaced unit to Garmin. A run or strength unit goes to Garmin only through the direct push and an outdoor ride through the Wahoo library plan push; a strength unit without a structure, or with an exercise that has no Garmin match, is never sent to Garmin and carries the reason in `push_blocked` (`no_structure` or `unmapped`). The calendar card says so ("Keine Kraftstruktur – nicht an Garmin gesendet") with "Mit Coach strukturieren"; an unmatched exercise shows up to five Garmin catalogue suggestions to pick with one tap (`POST /api/planned-units/<id>/garmin-exercise`, remembered for that exercise name) or "Andere…" for the Coach. The toast names what arrived where ("Garmin: 1 strukturiert · Intervals.icu: 2 gesendet"). Per provider the week sends its units, then its leftovers, as `plan_push` jobs of at most 28 entries, Intervals.icu last; each entry carries the unit's current payload hash, so a unit edited meanwhile is refused instead of sent. Only configured providers are offered, and Garmin is compared by the unit's name, date and structure or run text (so an Intervals.icu sync does not bring the button back). Units in a sync conflict, past units, units already matched to a recorded activity and completed activities are not sent.
- **Unidirectional Write Boundary**: Strict boundary preventing standard read syncs from altering, overwriting, or deleting locally maintained planned workouts.
- **Automatic metric export to Intervals.icu**: Automatic export is off by default. The athlete enables it once in the Garmin card (More → Anbindungen), below the Garmin login and only while Garmin is connected, confirming the named target; this consent is persisted in the encrypted database and can be revoked in the same place. Consent is stored per field (weight, body fat, resting heart rate, running threshold pace, threshold heart rate, maximum heart rate, cycling FTP, running FTP): a new consent starts with threshold heart rate, maximum heart rate and running threshold pace; cycling FTP (maintained by the athlete at Intervals.icu unless consented), running FTP and the wellness fields are opt-in. A consent given before per-field consent existed keeps the fields of that time (all but running FTP) until the athlete changes one, and existing per-field consent carries over. While enabled, each field has its own checkbox; every change is confirmed first and answered with a receipt naming the changed fields. Coach workouts use relative Intervals.icu steps, so the sport settings keep the workouts right on Garmin and Zwift: they receive the current resolved thresholds of the local zone configuration, else Garmin, else Wahoo (never a value Intervals.icu reported), and a different Intervals.icu value is overwritten. Heart-rate thresholds are written with `recalcHrZones=true`, so Intervals.icu recalculates its heart-rate zones; power and pace zones follow FTP and threshold pace there. Wellness fields receive provider-reported Garmin measurements of the last 90 days; existing differing wellness values without an accepted baseline are never overwritten, matching values establish a baseline, and a later change at Intervals.icu pauses that field until the value at Intervals.icu matches its last accepted value again. The export runs only while Intervals.icu is configured: after a successful current Garmin sync, a few seconds after a local zone configuration is saved, removed or undone, and after a successful Wahoo sync. Fixture, failed, partial and historical Garmin runs do not trigger exports. Each write is listed in the change history (type `metric_export`, source automatic; entries with source manual from an earlier release stay listed) without undo, because the write is remote. The Garmin card shows the last status line with sent values, conflicts and failures; export failures never undo the local import and retry on the next trigger. AI estimates and Intervals.icu values are never sent. Baselines survive restarts and backup/restore in the existing encrypted database; no schema change is needed. The Coach has no metric export tool.
- **Illness Pause Synchronization**: Explicit synchronization of confirmed illness pauses to Intervals.icu as `SICK` calendar events only upon dedicated athlete request.
- **Competition Sync**: Local competitions (dates, priorities A/B/C, target sports, goal times) are pushed to Intervals.icu on explicit synchronization; remote values never overwrite a clean local row.

## Garmin Connect Integration & Health Metrics
- **Direct Read-Only Health Sync**: Independent synchronization of Garmin Connect wellness metrics, activity recordings, and physiological measurements.
- **Official Personal API transport**: With a Garmin Personal API key, activities, activity details, sleep, HRV, daily summaries, resting heart rate, Body Battery, weight and heart-rate zones are read through Garmin's official read-only Personal API; every family it serves with the same content uses it. The unofficial login stays an optional supplement for what the Personal API does not serve: training readiness, training status, training load, load balance, VO2 max, FTP, lactate threshold, race predictions, personal records, endurance score, running tolerance, gear, activity splits and per-activity zone times. Activity details also download the original FIT file through the Personal API; its full-resolution record streams and device laps replace the downsampled charts and splits. Garmin's own zone times, training effect, altitude and temperature range come from the activity list; the RPE, feel and minimum heart rate rated on the watch come from one single-activity read per new activity and per activity of the last three days (at most 30 per sync). Each night's sleep record also gives the mean and lowest SpO2, the mean respiration rate and the mean sleeping heart rate (both transports); the Coach recovery section shows the latest night with each value's 14-day mean. Garmin's whole-day pulse-ox and respiration summaries are not imported. Either transport works alone; both together write each activity and day once. Without the login, the Personal API families still sync when the login fails or needs MFA; the Garmin card then asks to renew the Garmin login for the additional values. The card also warns 14 days before the entered key expiry date, and when the key has expired or was rejected. "Verbindung prüfen" exchanges the Personal API key first and checks the login only when it is connected; a rejection names the key or the login.
- **Strength workouts to the watch**: A dated strength planned unit with a `strength_structure` can be sent to Garmin Connect as a structured strength workout (one repeat group per exercise with reps, kg as the weight target, %1RM, RPE or RIR and the exercise note as the step note, and a timed rest; warm-up sets are their own repeat group of Garmin warm-up steps before the working sets) scheduled on its planned date. It is sent only by the explicit job `POST /api/sync/jobs` with `{"provider": "garmin", "type": "plan_push", "payload": {"entries": [{"library_workout_id": "<planned unit id>", "expected_payload_hash": "<preview hash>"}]}}`. The Coach queues the same push without a card when the athlete's own message asks to send the unit to Garmin or the watch (Intervals.icu wording never grants it), bound to the units as they were stored; an explicit removal asks on a card first. Nothing is sent automatically, and rides keep going through Intervals.icu: with Intervals.icu's Garmin option "upload planned workouts" on, limit it to rides, since strength and runs reach Garmin through this direct push and would otherwise arrive twice. Each exercise is matched to Garmin's exercise catalogue by its optional `garmin_exercise` (an exact English Garmin exercise name, checked when the unit is saved and stored with its category/exercise pair), then by a catalogue name already confirmed for the same exercise name in another local unit or template (so a confirmation is asked once), then by its name, ignoring spacing, hyphens and a plural s (Lat-Pulldown, Face Pulls), then by a German/English alias list (Kniebeuge, Schrägbankdrücken, Enges Rudern im Sitzen, Bizeps am Seilzug, …), then by its words when exactly one catalogue entry is closest. An unmatched exercise blocks that unit with up to five catalogue suggestions, because Garmin silently blanks unknown exercises; after the upload the saved workout is read back and deleted again if Garmin dropped an exercise or changed a warm-up step's type. A second push updates the same Garmin workout and moves its own calendar entry. An entry `{"library_workout_id": "<id>", "remove": true}` deletes only that workout and its schedule; other Garmin calendar items are never touched. Once a unit's day has passed, the next Garmin refresh deletes its workout and schedule from Garmin without a button (at most 20 per refresh), so the watch's workout list does not fill up; this is the only Garmin write without a request, and recorded activities and workouts the app did not send are never touched. Once (athlete decision 2026-11-12) the refresh also deletes the app's older strength workouts that lost their unit: only workouts whose description starts with the line "Pacecraft", that no unit of today or later links and that are not scheduled from today on, at most 20 per refresh until the library has been read through. Past, deleted, archived units and units that are neither strength nor run are rejected. The Garmin workout and schedule ids are kept in the local remote links. Writing needs the unofficial Garmin login (the Personal API is read-only): with the Personal API key alone, or after a skipped login, the calendar shows "Garmin-Anmeldung nötig" instead of the Garmin push and the Coach's Garmin push fails before a job is queued. The read-back also compares reps, sets and the weight in kg; a changed remembered exercise marks pushed units as changed; a retry reuses a schedule Garmin already holds for the workout and day. A Garmin outage stops the remaining units of the run instead of retrying.
- **Runs to the watch**: A future run unit with structured step text is sent to Garmin Connect as a structured running workout on its planned date, by the same explicit `plan_push` job as strength and under the same Coach rules (the tool `push_strength_to_garmin` takes strength and run units). Percent and zone targets are resolved to absolute values (watts, bpm, m/s) from the athlete's run zones on the unit's date, one snapshot per push run, and the heart-rate cap is sent as bpm. A run with a ramp or a pace unit other than /km or /mi is not offered for Garmin, and a capped run needs a known threshold heart rate. A later threshold change marks a pushed run whose percent, zone or cap steps now resolve differently as changed, so the sync button offers it again; nothing is sent without the athlete. Garmin now gets runs directly, so switch off Intervals.icu's Garmin upload for running, or each run arrives twice. Rides stay on Intervals.icu (Zwift) and the Wahoo library plan push.
- **Dedicated Sleep Gating**: Intelligent morning check-in gate that delays morning coaching until the current day's sleep analysis is processed and finalized by Garmin.
- **Overnight Body Battery Tracking**: Targeted extraction of resting stress metrics capturing the final level before sleep and the initial waking level within one hour of rising.
- **Bounded Sleep Retry Engine**: Fixed 15-minute retry, at most 3 attempts, handling delayed Garmin cloud sleep processing.
- **Garmin-Specific Metric Labeling**: Clear source labeling distinguishing Garmin-calculated FTP, running threshold power, threshold HR, threshold pace, and VO2 max from Wahoo values.
- **Daily Wellness Summaries**: Daily summaries tracking total steps, floors climbed, active calories, resting heart rate, and overnight HRV; `Analyse` charts steps and floors under `Belastung` and resting heart rate and HRV under `Erholung`. Garmin's dated daily energy expenditure is also shown alongside the nutrition diary, with current-day values marked provisional. For the running day, a separately labelled derived projection of the full-day total (resting calories of the last complete day plus active calories so far) is added.
- **Rolling Health Averages**: Automated calculation of recovery baselines for sleep, HRV and resting heart rate from earlier nights, shown as a reference range in `Analyse` → `Erholung`; HRV adds a 7-day rolling mean. The current performance values show the value only.
- **Transparent Metric Fallback**: Resilient metric aggregation preserving labeled fallbacks when the preferred source has no current value; the current performance values show the selected value only, and the Analyse charts name the source of each series.
- **Threshold selection**: Coach, current metrics, plans, zones, the local session load (M6), the Wahoo plan header, the Garmin export and the staleness hint read each threshold through one dated precedence, so they agree for the same day: a locally confirmed zone configuration in effect that day comes first; for the threshold heart rate the dated Garmin watch zone set in effect that day (its lactate threshold HR) comes second; the stored observations follow. Every value keeps its source label (local, Garmin, Wahoo). The current cycling FTP is then Garmin Connect's, then Wahoo's; threshold and maximum heart rate, running threshold pace and run power come from Garmin Connect's latest biometric readings (which can differ from manually configured watch sport profiles). Intervals.icu is output-only and never a source; a Garmin threshold counts for 180 days. Scaled threshold speed is normalized to m/s before canonical storage and displayed as min/km. FTP calendar dates accept Garmin's ISO date or datetime format and retain its calendar day. An undated latest cycling FTP uses the sync day as its observation day; undated FTP history is not invented. Mapper updates re-project stored provider records through the existing background backfill. The general maximum-heart-rate value shows the higher of the available running and cycling values from Garmin; threshold heart rate is a separate metric.

## Weather Intelligence & Outdoor Scheduling
- **Open-Meteo Integration**: Server-side cached 14-day local weather forecasts powered by the non-commercial Open-Meteo meteorological API.
- **Dual-Model Forecast Engine**: Intelligent forecast resolution using high-precision DWD ICON-D2 for short-range predictions and ECMWF IFS HRES for long-range outlooks.
- **Air Quality & Pollen**: Open-Meteo/CAMS European AQI, particulate matter, ozone, nitrogen dioxide, and UV index for about 5 days. For German locations the official DWD pollen-flight hazard index (0–3, today plus two days) is used for the automatically resolved pollen region; elsewhere Open-Meteo/CAMS pollen concentrations are the fallback.
- **Compact Calendar Weather**: Each calendar day shows only a weather symbol with its temperature range beside the date, so the sessions and their load/interval charts stay in the foreground; small flags are added only when notable (rain from 50 %, UV from 6, European AQI from 60, any DWD pollen level, CAMS pollen from 10 grains/m³), and all weather and air figures appear in the symbol's tooltip.
- **Weather Window Recommendations**: Automated calculation of optimal outdoor training windows over the next 5 days based on temperature, precipitation, wind speeds, and air quality.
- **Workday Schedule Awareness**: Outdoor suggestions use fixed clock windows, not a configurable working-hours setting: Monday-Thursday 05:00-06:00, 12:00-13:00 and 16:00-22:00, Friday 05:00-06:00, 12:00-13:00 and 14:00-22:00, Saturday and Sunday 06:00-21:00.
- **Adaptive Weather Reason**: An outdoor ride of at least 180 minutes today or in the next two days gets a reason for the adaptive replanning preview when the forecast shows persistent rain or snow. There is no heat or wind rule and no weather notification. The PWA notifies only about a competition within 3 days and a failed sync.
- **Weather During Sessions**: Each ride or run since 2022 that is outdoors, or whose environment is unknown but has a stored GPS start position, gets the Open-Meteo historical-forecast weather (temperature, apparent temperature, humidity, wind, precipitation) at its start position, or, for an outdoor session without a stored position, at the saved weather location, labelled as home; an unknown-environment session never uses the home. Only coordinates rounded to two decimals leave the server. A stored result is never fetched again; failed requests are retried later with a growing delay. The Coach sees the weather and a heat note from an apparent temperature of 25 °C, so heat is not read as a fitness change, never a coordinate.
- **Route Recognition**: Each ride or run with a stored GPS track that is not known to be indoor or virtual is fingerprinted by its start, its distance and the coarse 500 m grid cells (sampled every 150 m) it passes. Sessions are the same route when their distances differ by at most 10 %, the start (or, reversed, the end) lies in or beside the other's start cell and at least 60 % of the cells (Jaccard overlap) agree; direction does not matter. Repeats are numbered by start time. The activity view and the Coach (`route` block of `get_activity_details`) get only the label ("Strecke 3" until the athlete renames it with `POST /api/routes/rename`), the visit number, the visit count and an opaque id, never a coordinate or cell, so efforts on one loop are comparable. Sessions without a usable track get no route.
- **Efficient Server Caching**: 3-hour server-side forecast caching with automatic on-demand cache overrides accessible from the More tab.

## External Calendars & Schedule Constraints
- **Read-Only iCalendar (ICS) Sync**: Secure polling of private external iCalendar feeds (Google Calendar, Apple iCloud, Microsoft Outlook) without write permissions.
- **Rolling 8-Week Event Horizon**: Bounded calendar expansion mapping external life events across an 8-week (56-day) forward-looking window.
- **RFC 5545 Recurrence Engine**: Comprehensive expansion of standard recurring rules (daily, weekly, monthly, yearly) and Google Calendar recurrence exceptions capped at 1,000 instances. A date-only `UNTIL` includes recurrences on that day in the event's own time zone, and feeds whose recurrence expansion is too expensive are rejected. A component with an unsupported or invalid recurrence rule or missing UID/DTSTART is skipped instead of failing the feed; the sync result and the calendar status count the skips ("n Termine übersprungen", reason class only, no event text). A series whose `UNTIL` lies before the window is dropped silently. `DURATION` accepts weeks (`P1W`); property parameters may carry quoted colons.
- **Bounded Calendar Processing**: Feeds are limited to 5 MB and 10,000 parsed event components; folded lines, RDATE deduplication and recurrence-exception lookup are processed linearly. Instance records are generated incrementally and feeds exceeding 1,000 unique events in the sync window are rejected. Connected calendar HTTP transfers have a 30-second total deadline.
- **Personal Events in the Calendar**: From today on, every event of the feed shows as a chip above the day's units with its impact: Blockiert, Kürzer, Locker, Egal or Offen (no decision and no marker yet). A tap opens a sheet (a side panel on wide screens) to set the impact for this event or, for a recurring one, for every occurrence; the decision is stored locally, survives feed refreshes, overrides the markers and changes planning like the matching marker. The month view marks days with a blocked event. Undecided events from today through 14 days ahead raise a notice: "{n} neue Termine bis {date}" above the calendar with "Einordnen", which walks through them one by one, and the same count on the Kalender navigation item; it goes away once each is decided ("Egal" counts) and never returns for an event already decided.
- **Calendar Training Constraints**: `[NO_TRAINING]`, `[NO_INTENSITY]`, and `[SHORT_ONLY]` are recognized in event titles and descriptions. `[NO_TRAINING]` and `[NO_INTENSITY]` are enforced against planned-workout changes on the affected local calendar day: training is blocked for the former, the latter blocks hard workouts (the one hard-session rule shared by plan checks, adaptive replanning and race-proximity conflicts) and strength that is not explicitly easy, and `[SHORT_ONLY]` blocks workouts longer than 60 minutes.
- **Calendar-aware Coach planning**: Week and block planning include external events within 14 and 35 days and summarize occupied time without double-counting overlaps. The Coach asks whether to train or reduce intensity when an event makes the choice unclear. A long or hard session overlapping an event or falling on a day with at least eight occupied hours gets a non-blocking plan note; explicit calendar markers remain enforced.
- **Visual Schedule Conflict Markers**: Distinctive visual indicators on the planned calendar alerting the athlete to busy days and potential scheduling conflicts.
- **Adaptive Session Replanning**: Heuristic session adjustments that suggest shorter durations or lower-intensity replacements for scheduled workouts on congested days. A strength unit keeps its exercises, sets and weights and only gets the light strength intensity; a run or ride may become a generic easy replacement.
- **Stepwise Return After an Illness Pause**: After an approved pause window, three return stages follow: stage 1 easy sessions only, stage 2 easy and moderate, stage 3 the normal plan. Stage 1 starts the day after the pause; each stage lasts at least 2 days. Each morning the adaptive preview proposes at most one stage forward when the check-in of the day reports no pain or illness and a normal body feel (soreness up to 5 of 10), the overnight HRV (RMSSD) is not below the lower quartile of the athlete's own earlier nights (the personal recovery baseline: 42 days, at least 14 earlier nights, same measurement context) and the resting heart rate is at most 5 bpm above the median of those nights. A poor sign holds the stage; a new pain or illness complaint steps back one stage (an illness report starts a new pause forecast instead). If the Garmin night is missing or has too few earlier nights, the check-in alone decides; without today's check-in the stage holds. Evidence is shown per source (local check-in, Garmin). The preview also replaces drafts of today and tomorrow that exceed the stage (easy, at most 60 minutes), as local drafts only; nothing is written to Intervals.icu. A stage change applies only with the preview approval and is stored on the applied adjustment (no separate state). While a stage below 3 runs, the plan guard treats sessions above it like the illness rule (`illness_hard`). An unadvanced return ends after 28 days.

## Performance Analytics, Metrics & Trends
- **Historical Trends**: Trend lines for FTP, running threshold pace and VO2 max (last 12 calendar weeks), and for resting heart rate, heart rate variability (HRV) and body weight (14 days or 12 weeks).
- **Local Session Load**: Every session's TSS-scale load and intensity factor are computed locally with method M6 (`docs/performance-methods/m6-local-tss-load.md`): power against the FTP valid on that day, else run pace against the threshold pace, else heart rate as normalised Banister TRIMP (walking included) with the constants of the profile setting **Geschlecht** (male or female; not set uses the male constants, marked as an assumption in the Coach's session data). Strength takes its session-RPE load (elapsed minutes × RPE) times a personal TSS-per-AU factor: the median over the locally or Garmin-rated rides and runs of the last 365 days at a similar RPE (within 1 of the strength session's, else within 2), from at least 20 of them; unrated or uncalibrated strength gets none. Yoga and alpine ski get none. Provider loads (Intervals.icu, Wahoo, Garmin) are never summed. Values are stored per session and recomputed after each provider sync, local zone change, session correction, changed session RPE and change of a preferred threshold source or of the profile sex.
- **Impulse-Response Fitness Modeling**: Fitness, fatigue and form come only from the local curve (card M7, `docs/performance-methods/m7-local-fitness-fatigue.md`): the local TSS-scale session load smoothed with 42 and 7 days over the whole stored history, computed on read. The load tab, the Coach load block and training report, comparisons, the progress report and block review, the daily planning context and the load scenarios all read it; days whose sessions have no local load (for example unrated strength or yoga) are marked incomplete. Stored Intervals.icu CTL, ATL and form are not shown and never mixed into the local values. No ramp rate is derived.
- **Session Efficiency Metrics**: Each ride and run gets a local efficiency factor, variability index, mechanical work and aerobic decoupling (card M8, `docs/performance-methods/m8-local-session-metrics.md`), stored per session and shown in the activity detail and to the Coach. Work and decoupling need stored full-resolution streams; calories are the device estimate (Garmin, Wahoo). The values are descriptive: no fixed decoupling limit and no fitness verdict.
- **Running Dynamics and Form Drift**: With a sensor that measures them (running pod, HRM-Pro/Run strap or a watch), a run's activity detail shows the averages of ground contact time (ms), the share of it on the left foot (percent), vertical oscillation (cm), vertical ratio (percent) and step length (m); nothing shows when the device did not measure them. They come from the Garmin activity summary (`avgGroundContactTime`, `avgGroundContactBalance`, `avgVerticalOscillation`, `avgVerticalRatio`, `avgStrideLength`, where a 0 is "not measured") and as full-resolution series from the FIT record fields `stance_time`, `stance_time_balance`, `vertical_oscillation`, `vertical_ratio` and `step_length` (scales taken from the FIT SDK profile, not yet checked against a recorded file). A run of at least 45 minutes outside trails also gets a form drift check: the first and the last third are compared when their speed differs by at most 5 %, each third has measured data for at least half its time, and a ground contact time that rose by at least 5 % shows "Laufstil lässt nach". Vertical oscillation (+10 %) and step length (-5 %) are reported against their own thresholds without flagging the run. These thresholds are heuristics of this app, not validated cut-offs, and the check is no injury-risk score. The Coach receives the summary values and the drift result, never the series.
- **Race Prediction Engine**: Garmin Connect race predictions for 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon, shown in the running section.
- **Sport-Specific Intensity Distributions**: Heart rate, power and run pace zone distribution analysis across running, outdoor cycling, and indoor/virtual cycling activities; the Analysis load tab draws them as HF-, Power- and Pace-Zonen below the training focus. Reports count each session's seconds from its streams with the local zones in effect on the session day (stored with the session analysis); a session without streams shows its Garmin watch zones in a separate "(Gerät)" distribution, never added to local zone seconds. Intervals.icu zone times are not read.
- **Nutritional Intake Logging**: Integrated tracking of daily caloric intake and macronutrient splits (protein, carbohydrates, fats) stored alongside training load.
- **Explicit Metric Freshness**: Clear differentiation between observation timestamps and synchronization retrieval times to prevent outdated metrics appearing fresh.

## Privacy, Security & Data Management
- **SQLCipher AES-256 Encryption**: Complete encryption of all athlete data, metrics, database-stored provider credentials (API keys and secrets entered in the UI), provider tokens (Wahoo and Garmin), chat history, and attachments at rest using `APP_PASSWORD`. Only a legacy Garmin token file, if one still exists, is plaintext and not covered; see [setup](setup.md#garmin-authentication--mfa-setup).
- **Database Key Placement**: Keep `APP_PASSWORD` in the container environment or the project-root `.env`. If it is found in `DATA_DIR/.env`, beside the encrypted database, startup logs a warning (never the value), because a copy of the data directory would then include its key.
- **Strict Session Security**: High-security session cookies hardened with `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict`, and optional `Secure` flags.
- **Bounded HTTP Input**: At most 32 active handlers, with a 20-second limit for the request headers and a separate 20-second limit for each 1 MiB piece of a request body, so a slow but steady upload is not cut off. Login admission is checked before reading its body; these input limits do not shorten Coach responses or SSE streams.
- **Zero Third-Party Trackers**: Self-hosted architecture containing zero tracking scripts, third-party analytics, external CDNs, or telemetry reporting.
- **Comprehensive Privacy Export**: Single-click export producing a complete, unencrypted ZIP archive of JSON and JSON Lines files of all profile records, workouts, metrics, and chat history.
- **Confirmed Local Data Purge**: Irreversible deletion of all local athlete data, including stored provider credentials and Garmin tokens, guarded by an explicit typed confirmation phrase (`LOKALE DATEN LÖSCHEN`). The Garmin account and Intervals.icu/Wahoo accounts themselves are not changed, but the app's Wahoo grant is revoked at Wahoo first (best effort; a failure is reported and does not stop the purge).
- **Zero-Downtime Maintenance Mode**: Process-level maintenance gate that prevents concurrent writes and ensures transaction safety during database restoration.
- **Pre-Restore Rollback Copies**: Automated creation of a safety copy of the existing database before executing any database restore or replacement.
- **Redacted Operational Logging**: Structured server logs that correlate technical operation IDs while stripping authentication headers, tokens, and athlete text.
- **Always-on Technical Diagnostics**: Bounded diagnostic logger (up to 10,000 entries from the last 7 days, 2 MiB total, 512 KiB per entry) recording sanitized OpenAI request bodies, response and non-OpenAI request shapes (field names, types, lengths, no values), provider error details, tool activity, and error traces. Credentials, keys, tokens, and session data are removed before storage. New entries are written to the database in one batch at most 30 seconds after they are captured, as background work, and on shutdown, including `docker stop`; a crash or forced kill can lose the entries of the last 30 seconds.
